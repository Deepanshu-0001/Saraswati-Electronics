Frequently Asked Questions

Answers about common TV faults, diagnosis, repair enquiries, spare parts and what information to share before a technician checks the TV.

TV Repair

Repair enquiries can cover LED, LCD, Smart TV and Android TV models. The actual repair depends on the brand, model, fault, part availability and inspection findings.

Send the TV brand, exact model number, screen size and a clear description of the symptom. A short photo or video can help explain display, sound, startup or connectivity problems.

Often, yes, but the cause must be diagnosed. Backlight strips, the power/backlight driver, main board, display connections or the panel can produce similar symptoms.

Power and startup faults are common repair enquiries. First rule out the wall socket, power lead and external devices; internal power-board or main-board diagnosis may then be needed.

Software, app, boot-loop, Wi-Fi and remote-control symptoms can be checked. Some faults are software-related while others come from storage, power or main-board hardware.
Display & Picture

Lines can come from panel faults, display connections, T-Con circuitry on applicable models, or board-level problems. A visual symptom alone is not enough to identify the exact part.

Low brightness settings should be ruled out first. If the picture is faint even at normal brightness, backlight LEDs, driver circuitry, power supply or the panel may need diagnosis.

Flicker can be caused by signal/source issues, backlight problems, power instability, board faults or panel problems. Testing the source and inputs first helps narrow it down.
Power & Sound

Check mute, volume, audio output settings and external devices first. If picture works normally but audio does not, speaker, audio circuit or main-board diagnosis may be required.

Restart loops can be linked to software, storage, power supply, overheating or main-board faults. The exact cause depends on the model and behaviour during startup.

Try another cable, source device and HDMI port first. If the problem follows one TV port or none of the ports respond, the input section or main board may need inspection.
Smart TV & Connectivity

Restart the TV and router, forget and reconnect to the network, and confirm other devices connect normally. Persistent TV-only failures can involve software or Wi-Fi hardware.
